# Module 2 - Security Frameworks and Controls

## Overview

Cybersecurity is not just about using firewalls or antivirus software. Organizations need structured approaches to identify risks, protect valuable assets, comply with legal requirements, and respond to security incidents effectively. This module introduces the concepts of **security frameworks**, **security controls**, and organizational governance that help build strong cybersecurity programs.

Frameworks provide organizations with a roadmap for managing security, while controls reduce the likelihood and impact of cyber threats. This module also explains the role of security policies, standards, procedures, and guidelines, which together ensure that security practices are applied consistently across an organization.

By completing this module, I gained a deeper understanding of how organizations build security programs, reduce risks, and continuously improve their cybersecurity posture.

---

# What is a Security Framework?

A security framework is a structured set of best practices, recommendations, and guidelines that helps organizations manage cybersecurity risks and improve their security posture.

Instead of creating security programs from scratch, organizations adopt recognized frameworks to ensure that security activities follow industry standards and proven practices.

Security frameworks help organizations:

- Identify risks
- Protect critical assets
- Detect security incidents
- Respond effectively
- Recover from cyber attacks
- Improve security continuously

Frameworks provide consistency, improve decision-making, and simplify compliance with legal and regulatory requirements.

---

# Why Security Frameworks are Important

Organizations operate in increasingly complex environments where cyber threats evolve constantly. Security frameworks help by providing a repeatable process for managing risks instead of reacting to incidents only after they occur.

Benefits include:

- Better risk management
- Improved security planning
- Consistent security practices
- Regulatory compliance
- Improved incident response
- Stronger organizational security culture

Without a framework, security efforts become inconsistent and difficult to manage.

---

# NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

One of the most widely used cybersecurity frameworks is the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)** developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology.

The framework helps organizations manage cybersecurity risks using five core functions.

---

## 1. Identify

Understand what needs protection.

Activities include:

- Asset inventory
- Risk assessment
- Business environment analysis
- Governance
- Asset management

Organizations cannot protect assets they do not know they have.

---

## 2. Protect

Implement safeguards to reduce cybersecurity risks.

Examples include:

- Access control
- Encryption
- Security awareness training
- Data protection
- Secure configuration

The Protect function focuses on preventing incidents before they occur.

---

## 3. Detect

Identify cybersecurity events as quickly as possible.

Detection methods include:

- Log monitoring
- SIEM platforms
- Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)
- Continuous monitoring
- Threat intelligence

Early detection minimizes the impact of attacks.

---

## 4. Respond

Take action after detecting a security incident.

Response activities include:

- Incident response
- Threat containment
- Communication
- Investigation
- Documentation

Effective response reduces damage and speeds recovery.

---

## 5. Recover

Restore normal business operations after an incident.

Recovery includes:

- Restoring systems
- Recovering backups
- Lessons learned
- Improving future defenses
- Business continuity

Recovery ensures organizations can continue operating after cyber incidents.

# Security Controls

Security controls are safeguards implemented to reduce cybersecurity risks and protect organizational assets.

Controls are designed to prevent, detect, or respond to threats.

Security controls generally fall into three categories.

---

## Physical Controls

These protect physical resources and facilities.

Examples:

- Security guards
- CCTV cameras
- Biometric authentication
- Access cards
- Locked server rooms
- Fencing

Physical controls reduce unauthorized physical access.

---

## Technical Controls

Technical controls use technology to improve security.

Examples:

- Firewalls
- Antivirus software
- Encryption
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)
- Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS)

These controls help defend against technical attacks.

---

## Administrative Controls

Administrative controls consist of policies and management practices.

Examples:

- Security awareness training
- Password policies
- Risk assessments
- Incident response plans
- Employee onboarding
- Acceptable use policies

Administrative controls guide employee behavior and organizational processes.

---

# Types of Security Controls

Security controls can also be classified by their purpose.

### Preventive Controls

Designed to stop attacks before they happen.

Examples:

- Firewalls
- MFA
- Access control
- Security training

---

### Detective Controls

Help identify security incidents.

Examples:

- SIEM
- Log monitoring
- IDS
- Security audits

---

### Corrective Controls

Reduce the impact after an incident occurs.

Examples:

- Backups
- Patch management
- Malware removal
- Disaster recovery

---

### Deterrent Controls

Discourage attackers from attempting attacks.

Examples:

- Warning banners
- Security cameras
- Security policies

---

### Compensating Controls

Alternative controls used when primary controls cannot be implemented.

Example:

If MFA cannot be deployed immediately, stronger password policies and network restrictions may temporarily reduce risk.

---

# Security Policies

A security policy is a high-level document that defines an organization's security objectives and expectations.

Policies answer **"What must be done?"**

Examples include:

- Password Policy
- Acceptable Use Policy
- Remote Work Policy
- Data Protection Policy

Policies are approved by management and apply across the organization.

---

# Security Standards

Standards define mandatory technical requirements that support security policies.

Standards answer **"What requirements must be followed?"**

Examples:

- Minimum password length
- Encryption algorithms
- Device configuration standards

Unlike guidelines, standards are mandatory.

---

# Security Procedures

Procedures provide step-by-step instructions for completing security-related tasks.

Examples:

- Creating user accounts
- Responding to phishing emails
- Installing software updates
- Performing backups

Procedures ensure consistency across the organization.

---

# Security Guidelines

Guidelines provide recommended best practices.

Unlike policies and standards, guidelines are generally flexible rather than mandatory.

Examples:

- Tips for creating strong passwords
- Secure browsing recommendations
- Email security advice

Guidelines help employees make informed security decisions.

---

# Security Audits

A security audit evaluates whether an organization follows its security policies, standards, and regulatory requirements.

Audits help identify:

- Missing controls
- Compliance issues
- Configuration weaknesses
- Security gaps

Regular audits improve organizational security.

---

# Compliance

Organizations often operate under legal or industry regulations.

Examples include:

- GDPR
- HIPAA
- PCI DSS
- ISO/IEC 27001

Compliance demonstrates that organizations follow required security practices and protect sensitive information appropriately.

---

# Risk Management

Risk management is an ongoing process of identifying, evaluating, and reducing cybersecurity risks.

The basic steps include:

1. Identify assets
2. Identify threats
3. Identify vulnerabilities
4. Assess risk
5. Implement controls
6. Monitor continuously

Risk management supports informed business decisions.

---

# Defense in Depth

Defense in Depth uses multiple security layers instead of relying on a single control.

Examples include:

- Firewalls
- MFA
- Encryption
- Security awareness
- Endpoint protection
- Network monitoring
- Backups

If one control fails, additional layers continue protecting the organization.

---

# Security Governance

Security governance ensures that cybersecurity supports organizational goals.

It includes:

- Leadership involvement
- Risk management
- Policy development
- Compliance
- Accountability
- Continuous improvement

Good governance aligns cybersecurity with business objectives.
